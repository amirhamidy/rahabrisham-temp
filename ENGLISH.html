  • Broiler Breeder Farm

    With population growth and increasing demand for protein products, the poultry farming industry has become one of the important pillars of the country's food security. Meanwhile, supplying high-quality and resistant day-old chicks directly impacts the performance of broiler and layer farms. "The Largest Broiler Breeder Farm and Day-Old Chick Production in the Country in Khoshab" is a national and strategic project designed to meet domestic needs and develop the country's export capacity. This farm will be established in the industrial town of Khoshab County and, utilizing up-to-date technical knowledge and advanced infrastructure, will play an important role in supplying the country's chicken meat and egg production chain.

    Necessity and Importance of the Project

    Iran is one of the largest producers of chicken meat in the region, but supplying high-quality day-old chicks has always been one of the fundamental challenges of this industry. The dependence of some farms on imported day-old chicks and price fluctuations have created numerous problems for producers. In these circumstances, establishing a large and modern farm for producing resistant, healthy, and standard day-old chicks not only meets domestic needs but also provides the possibility of developing exports to regional countries.

    Benefits of implementing this project:

    1. Reducing dependence on imported day-old chicks and meeting the needs of domestic farms.

    2. Increasing the efficiency of the poultry industry by producing resistant and standard chicks.

    3. Creating sustainable employment in the Khoshab region and local economic development.

    4. Increasing Iran's competitiveness in regional and international markets.

    5. Improving the country's food security and reducing market fluctuations for chicken and eggs.

    Project Objectives

    Short-term Objectives:

    1. Establishing a modern and industrial complex for raising broiler breeders.

    2. Equipping the farm with advanced systems for controlling environmental and hygienic conditions.

    3. Producing day-old chicks with improved breeds resistant to diseases.

    4. Setting up a quality control laboratory and genetic management systems to improve the performance of parent flocks.

    Long-term Objectives:

    1. Supplying about 30% of the domestic need for day-old chicks.

    2. Developing the export capacity of day-old chicks to regional countries such as Iraq, Afghanistan, Turkmenistan, and other Persian Gulf countries.

    3. Reducing the production costs of chicken meat and eggs by increasing the quality of produced chicks.

    4. Creating a sustainable production chain including the broiler breeder farm, hatchery unit, and dedicated feed production plant.

    5. Participating in breed improvement research and developing new poultry strains to enhance performance and reduce feed consumption.

    Technical Specifications of the Project

    Production Capacity:

    1. Over 500,000 broiler breeders per cycle.

    2. Annual production of about 100 million day-old chicks.

    Project Infrastructure:

    1. Required Land: Over 20 hectares of land in the Khoshab industrial town.

    2. Rearing Halls: Several halls equipped with smart ventilation, heating, and cooling systems.

    3. Hatchery Units: With high production capacity and use of advanced hatchery machines.

    4. Genetics and Quality Control Laboratory: For monitoring health and improving the breed characteristics of parent stock.

    5. Management and Training Section: To train specialized personnel in poultry farming management.

    Technologies Used

    1. Automation Systems: Using automated systems for controlling temperature, humidity, and ventilation in rearing halls.

    2. Advanced Hatchery Machines: These machines provide optimal conditions for embryo development with high precision.

    3. Smart Management: Using management software to monitor the performance of parent stock and chicks.

    4. Environmental Technologies: Air purification and waste management systems to reduce pollution.

    Day-Old Chick Production Process

    Producing day-old chicks is a multi-stage process that starts from selecting parent stock to transferring chicks to the market.

    1. Selection and Rearing of Parent Stock:

    Selecting high-performance breeds with suitable genetic resistance and superior production efficiency.

    Rearing parent stock under fully standard and monitored conditions.

    Adjusting feeding and health programs to improve the quality of hatching eggs.

    2. Collection and Storage of Hatching Eggs:

    Daily collection of hatching eggs from parent stock halls.

    Storage under standard temperature and humidity conditions to maintain hatchability.

    3. Hatching Process:

    Placing hatching eggs in setter and hatcher machines.

    Controlling temperature, humidity, and ventilation to increase hatch rate.

    Monitoring the health and quality of chicks before transfer to the market.

    4. Transfer and Distribution of Day-Old Chicks:

    Standard packaging and transportation of chicks to broiler and layer farms.

    Providing quality assurance to ensure the health and proper performance of chicks.

    Market Analysis and Competition

    Given the increasing demand for chicken meat and eggs, the production of day-old chicks is of particular importance.

    Currently, many domestic farms depend on limited domestic producers or imports, which creates problems such as price fluctuations and health restrictions.

    Competitive Advantages of the Khoshab Farm:

    1. Using improved breeds resistant to diseases.

    2. Increasing production capacity and meeting domestic and export needs.

    3. Utilizing advanced hatching technologies and reducing losses.

    4. Strict quality control and health monitoring to increase the efficiency of produced chicks.

    Economic and Employment Benefits

    1. Direct employment for over 100 people in production, hatchery, and management sectors.

    2. Indirect employment for over 500 people in transportation, distribution, and sales sectors.

    3. Increasing the efficiency of the country's poultry industry and reducing production costs.

    4. Maintaining and strengthening Iran's position in regional markets.

    Challenges and Solutions

    1. Disease Control: Establishing standard quarantines and carefully monitoring flock health.

    2. Supply of Livestock Inputs: Producing part of the poultry feed within the domestic supply chain.

    3. Market Fluctuations: Concluding stable contracts with domestic and foreign producers.

    Conclusion

    The "Largest Broiler Breeder and Day-Old Chick Farm in the Country in Khoshab" project is one of the most important steps in developing Iran's poultry industry. With the establishment of this farm, the production capacity of day-old chicks will significantly increase, and domestic needs will be fully met. Additionally, the country's export capacity will be strengthened, and Iran can become one of the major regional exporters in the field of day-old chicks.

    1. Increased efficiency in the poultry industry.

    2. Creation of sustainable employment.

    3. Reduced dependence on imports.

    4. Development of exports and foreign currency earnings for the country.

    Implementing this project will bring about a fundamental transformation in Iran's poultry industry and further strengthen the country's food security.

  • Light & Heavy Livestock Refinery

    With population growth and lifestyle changes, the need for sustainable and hygienic supply of red meat and livestock by-products is felt more than ever. In this regard, establishing a light and heavy livestock refinery with a capacity of 1000 head of light livestock and 100 head of heavy livestock is a significant step towards optimizing the meat production chain, reducing waste, increasing efficiency, and maximizing the use of livestock by-products.

    This refinery, in addition to slaughtering and packaging meat, has specialized units for cutting and packaging, bone gelatin production, blood and plasma processing, suture thread production from intestines, skin and wool processing, and sausage and cold cut production. This innovative project, utilizing modern technologies, health standards, and new industrial management methods, will play an important role in increasing the efficiency of the livestock industry and reducing the country's dependence on imported livestock by-products.

    1. Necessity of Establishing a Light and Heavy Livestock Refinery

    Improving the supply chain of meat and livestock by-products.

    Optimal use of waste and converting it into valuable products.

    Reducing the country's dependence on imported gelatin, blood powder, suture thread, and other livestock by-products.

    Increasing the economic efficiency of livestock farming through complete animal processing.

    Increasing employment and industrial development in the region.

    Currently, many livestock wastes in Iran are discarded without proper processing, while in developed countries, these materials are used to produce high value-added products such as gelatin, suture thread, blood powder, and sausages and cold cuts. The livestock refinery, by creating a complete production cycle, converts all parts of the animal into valuable products.

    2. Capacity and Different Sections of the Refinery

    Production Capacity:

    Daily slaughter and processing of 1000 head of light livestock and 100 head of heavy livestock.

    Annual meat production capacity: Over 20,000 tons.

    Production of high value-added by-products from livestock waste.

    Main Sections of the Refinery:

    1. Meat Cutting and Packaging Unit

    2. Deboning and Bone Gelatin Production Unit

    3. Blood Processing Unit: Plasma and blood powder production

    4. Intestine Processing Unit: Medical suture thread production

    5. Sausage and Cold Cut Production Unit

    6. Skin and Wool Processing Unit

    3. Processing Details in Different Refinery Sections

    3.1. Meat Cutting and Packaging Unit

    . Carcass breakdown and hygienic packaging of red meat.

    Increasing meat shelf life through Vacuum Packaging.

    Adding value by supplying meat in various packages for domestic and export markets.

    3.2. Deboning and Bone Gelatin Production Unit

    . Processing animal bones and converting them into edible and industrial gelatin.

    Use in food (desserts, sweets) and pharmaceutical (gelatin capsules) industries.

    Replacing imported gelatin with domestic production.

    3.3. Blood Processing Unit: Plasma and Blood Powder Production

    Extracting plasma from animal blood for use in pharmaceutical and food industries.

    Producing blood powder for use in animal feed (rich protein source).

    3.4. Intestine Processing Unit: Medical Suture Thread Production

    Processing animal intestines and converting them into natural suture thread for medical use.

    Replacing imported natural suture thread and developing the medical equipment industry.

    3.5. Sausage and Cold Cut Production Unit

    Using red meat to produce processed meat products.

    High hygiene standards and production of products without harmful preservatives.

    3.6. Skin and Wool Processing Unit

    Converting animal hides into natural leather for the bag, shoe, and clothing industries.

    Processing wool for use in textile industries and yarn production.

    4. Economic and Industrial Advantages of the Livestock Refinery

    4.1. Waste Reduction and Increased Efficiency

    Converting all parts of the animal into sellable products, reducing resource waste.

    Increasing income for livestock farmers through complete animal processing.

    4.2. Reduced Dependence on Imports and Export Development

    Replacing imports of gelatin, blood powder, suture thread, and processed leather.

    Increasing exports of packaged meat products.

    4.3. Job Creation and Regional Development

    Direct and indirect employment for hundreds of people in the region.

    Development of the livestock processing industry in the country.

    5. Potential Challenges and Solutions

    Challenges:

    Difficulties in sourcing live animals for daily slaughter.

    Need for high initial investment to set up the refinery.

    Developing export markets for livestock by-products.

    Solutions:

    Creating a supply chain for light and heavy livestock from domestic farmers.

    Attracting public and private investment to finance the project.

    International marketing and cooperation with exporting companies.

    Conclusion

    The light and heavy livestock refinery with a capacity of 1000 head of light livestock and 100 head of heavy livestock is a national project aimed at modernizing the livestock industry, increasing efficiency, and reducing livestock waste. Production of high value-added by-products such as gelatin, suture thread, blood powder, leather, and sausages and cold cuts not only supplies the domestic market but also has high export potential.

    This refinery can serve as an industrial model for other regions of the country and contribute to the sustainable development of the livestock farming and processing industry.

    By implementing this project, Iran can become one of the main players in the region for producing meat products and by-products.

  • Medicinal Plant Refinery

    Iran, with over 8,000 plant species, is considered one of the richest countries in the world in terms of medicinal plants. North Khorasan province, with its diverse climate and suitable weather conditions, is one of the potential hubs for medicinal plant production in the country. However, the lack of modern processing infrastructure means that a significant portion of these plants are exported raw or are lost due to the absence of proper storage and processing methods.

    Given the increasing demand from the pharmaceutical, cosmetic, health, and food industries for extracts and active compounds of medicinal plants, establishing the first modern medicinal plant refinery in North Khorasan province can play a vital role in transforming this industry, increasing efficiency, and creating high added value in this field.

    1. Necessity of Establishing a Medicinal Plant Refinery

    Increasing efficiency from the country's medicinal plant capacities.

    Preventing the raw sale of medicinal plants and converting them into final products.

    Increasing the quality and standardization of herbal products for domestic and export markets.

    Creating sustainable employment and economic development in the region.

    Reducing the country's dependence on imported herbal extracts and active compounds.

    Currently, many herbal products needed by the pharmaceutical and cosmetic industries in Iran are imported. This refinery can meet domestic demand and turn Iran into one of the main players in the medicinal plant industry in the region.

    2. Capacity and Different Sections of the Refinery

    Production Capacity:

    Annual processing of over 5,000 tons of medicinal plants.

    Extraction and production of over 100 types of herbal extracts and active compounds.

    Production of diverse products for the pharmaceutical, cosmetic, health, and food industries.

    Main Sections of the Refinery:

    1. Extraction Unit for Active Herbal Ingredients

    2. Distillation and Separation Unit for Active Herbal Compounds

    3. Drying and Processing Unit for Medicinal Plants

    4. Natural Essential Oil Production Unit

    5. Packaging and Standardization Unit for Herbal Products

    6. Research and Development (R&D) Unit for process optimization and new product development

    3. Processing Details in Different Refinery Sections

    3.1. Extraction Unit for Active Herbal Ingredients

    Extracting active compounds from medicinal plants using modern methods such as solvent, water, steam, and ultrasound extraction.

    Maintaining the quality of active ingredients using controlled temperatures and optimized processes.

    Application in the production of herbal medicines, food supplements, and cosmetic products.

    3.2. Distillation and Separation Unit for Active Herbal Compounds

    Extracting volatile oils and aromatic compounds from medicinal plants.

    Producing natural essential oils for the food, cosmetic, and pharmaceutical industries.

    3.3. Drying and Processing Unit for Medicinal Plants

    Using modern drying technologies to preserve the therapeutic properties of plants.

    Standard packaging and storage to prevent quality degradation.

    3.4. Natural Essential Oil Production Unit

    Extracting high-purity essential oils for application in various industries.

    Processing aromatic plants such as lavender, damask rose, chamomile, mint, and thyme.

    4. Economic and Industrial Advantages of the Medicinal Plant Refinery

    4.1. Added Value and Increased Efficiency

    Converting raw medicinal plants into high-value processed products.

    Increasing income for farmers and collectors of medicinal plants.

    4.2. Reduced Dependence on Imports and Export Development

    Substituting imports of herbal extracts, essential oils, and active compounds.

    Increasing exports of standardized and high-quality herbal products.

    4.3. Job Creation and Regional Development

    Direct and indirect employment for hundreds of people in the region.

    Development of the medicinal plant processing industry in the country.

    5. Potential Challenges and Solutions

    Challenges:

    Ensuring a consistent supply of quality raw medicinal plants.

    Need for high initial investment for advanced processing technology.

    Competition in domestic and international markets for herbal products.

    Solutions:

    Establishing contract farming agreements with local medicinal plant growers.

    Securing funding through government support, private investment, and financial institutions.

    Focusing on quality control, standardization, and international marketing efforts.

    Conclusion

    The Medicinal Plant Refinery in North Khorasan province is a strategic project aimed at leveraging Iran's rich biodiversity, increasing industrial efficiency, and reducing reliance on imported herbal products. Production of high value-added extracts, essential oils, and active compounds will not only supply domestic industries but also holds significant potential for export.

    This refinery can serve as a model for sustainable development in the medicinal plant sector, contributing to both economic growth and the preservation of traditional knowledge.

    By implementing this project, Iran can solidify its position as a key player in the global medicinal and aromatic plants market.

  • Laying Hen Complex

    Given the importance of protein supply and the role of eggs as one of the most important nutritional sources in the household basket, establishing the largest laying hen complex in the country with a capacity of one million birds and daily production of one million eggs is a fundamental step towards meeting domestic needs, reducing import dependency, and developing export capacities. This complex, to be built in Khoshab County, as one of the largest and most modern egg production farms in the region, will adhere to global standards in production, hygiene, and packaging.

    This report examines various aspects of this project, including its importance, objectives, production process, market analysis, infrastructure, challenges, and economic benefits.

    Necessity and Importance of the Project

    Eggs, as one of the cheapest and richest sources of protein, play a vital role in the country's food security. According to statistics, Iran produces over one million tons of eggs annually and is among the major producers of this product in the region. However, we still face challenges such as price fluctuations, supply shortages, dependency of some regions on imports, and the need to improve production quality.

    Benefits of implementing this project:

    1. Increasing domestic production capacity and preventing egg shortages in the country.

    2. Eliminating import dependency and increasing Iran's export potential.

    3. Reducing the final cost of eggs and supplying market needs at a reasonable price.

    4. Creating sustainable employment for hundreds of people in the Khoshab region.

    5. Increasing the quality of produced eggs by adhering to global health standards.

    6. Using modern technologies to reduce losses and increase efficiency.

    Project Objectives

    Short-term Objectives:

    1. Establishing a modern industrial complex with a capacity of one million laying hens.

    2. Daily production of one million high-quality eggs.

    3. Creating standard poultry farming infrastructure including automated halls, advanced ventilation, and optimal feeding systems.

    4. Reducing production costs through optimal management of feed, energy, and human resources.

    Long-term Objectives:

    1. Becoming the main hub for supplying eggs in the country and the region.

    2. Developing export capacity to neighboring countries such as Iraq, Afghanistan, Persian Gulf countries, and Central Asia.

    3. Creating a sustainable supply chain including pullet rearing (young hens), a dedicated feed factory, and a packaging and distribution unit.

    4. Upgrading production standards and reducing common diseases in poultry farms.

    5. Participating in genetic research and improving laying hen breeds to increase production efficiency.

    Technical Specifications of the Project

    Production Capacity:

    1. One million laying hens per production cycle.

    2. Annual production of about 365 million eggs.

    3. Packaging and distribution of the product in domestic and foreign markets.

    Project Infrastructure:

    1. Required Land: Over 25 hectares in the Khoshab industrial town.

    2. Rearing Halls: Several modern halls with automated systems for ventilation, heating, cooling, and feeding.

    3. Feed Production Unit: Dedicated factory for supplying standard and high-quality feed.

    4. Quality Control Laboratory: To monitor the health and quality of produced eggs.

    5. Packaging and Warehousing Section: Equipped with automated packaging systems and cold storage.

    6. Distribution and Transportation Unit: For fast and optimal delivery of eggs to different parts of the country.

    Egg Production Process

    Egg production is a complex, multi-stage process involving breed selection, nutrition, health management, collection, packaging, and distribution.

    1. Selection and Rearing of Pullets (Young Hens):

  • Announcing the Reason for Persistent Inflation in Iran's Economy

    What curse is strangling Iran's economy?

    Zahra Aliakbari - Iran's economy has faced double-digit inflation for many years, to the extent that some estimates suggest Iran has the longest-lasting inflation in the world.

    Iran has faced inflation since the early 1350s SH (1970s CE), and this inflation has always been reported high.

    According to this report, in all these years, Iran's inflation rate has only become single-digit in a few years, and in other years, the inflation rate has always been double-digit. The important point is that in recent years, the nature of inflation has undergone a significant change. The inflation rate has now entered the forty percent channel. This high inflation rate has led to the weakening of the middle class's living conditions.

    What is the main reason for inflation in Iran?

    What is the main reason for inflation in Iran? This is a question that many experts have answered in previous years. The inflation rate in Iran is high due to fiscal and monetary indiscipline, which is manifested in the budget deficit. The budget deficit is an inseparable part of annual budgets in Iran.

    According to this report, the first budget bill drafted in Iran in the late Qajar period also faced a budget deficit.

    The first official budget in Iran's history, related to the Ministry of Sani-ol-Dowleh Hedayat in 1299 SH (approx. 1920 CE). Its revenues were estimated at 14,116,600 Tomans and its expenditures at 14,618,000 Tomans, and the interesting point is that Iran's first budget also had a deficit, amounting to 500,000 Tomans. The main part of its revenue consisted of taxes from the agricultural sector.

    The latest budget drafted in Iran, namely the budget for the year 1403, still faces a budget deficit. Although the government believes that changes made by the parliament to the budget bill are the cause of the four hundred trillion Toman budget deficit, the 1403 budget bill was rejected once by the Islamic Consultative Assembly based on its implicit budget deficit.

    The solutions that the government must implement to overcome the budget deficit often result in nothing but inflation. Although in recent years the government does not implement borrowing from the Central Bank due to legal prohibition, the path of this borrowing has become indirect, and the result of this action indirectly manifests in the economy.

    Printing money, issuing bonds, tasking banks to cover government expenses, covering the deficit by selling shares, etc., all result in nothing but inflation for the economy.

    When the government is large

    Mehdi Pazouki considers the budget deficit a result of the country's size. In an interview with the news agency, he emphasized: We run the country expensively. This expensive administration causes Iran's economy to face a serious crisis. We can turn off the inflation-producing machine in Iran with fiscal and monetary discipline, but as long as our estimate differs from our performance, we face this situation.

    He said: Unfortunately, despite the existing constraints, we still face the issue that the country's money is spent where it should not be. While fiscal discipline, alongside an accurate assessment of Iran's economic capacity, can solve the problem.

    Pazouki's reference is to a strange kind of idealism that the Iranian government faces. This ambition and far-fetched dreams can also be tracked and traced in development plans. A multitude of goals are formulated in development plans, but in practice, none come to fruition. Curbing inflation and entering single-digit inflation is also among these goals, which despite the start of the seventh development plan in Iran, has not yet yielded results.

    Morteza Afgheh, a faculty member at Shahid Chamran University, also confirmed this theory in an interview with Khabaronline news agency, saying that the Iranian government is excessively large, and a multitude of loss-making companies alongside the uneconomical management of companies and disregard for economic structures have paved the way for inflation.

    Kamran Nadri, an economics professor at Imam Sadiq University, also believes that eliminating inflation requires a mechanism that is not yet visible in Iran's economy.

    He believes that economic signals should indicate the government's plan to curb inflation. These signals include controlling the budget deficit, restoring fiscal and monetary discipline, and also adjusting the budget based on economic capacity.

    It seems that as long as the curse of the budget deficit strangles Iran's economy, inflation in Iran will remain high.
